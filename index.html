01 · THE SPINE

The gap nobody closes

There are two libraries. In one, fifty years of peer-reviewed research on how humans actually think, choose, trust, and buy. We have known for decades that people don't weigh probabilities, they feel them; that the first number you hear quietly anchors every number after it; that the people who adopt a new product first look nothing like your average customer. Whole fields of it, replicated, sitting in plain sight.

In the other library, the marketing playbook every founder gets handed: best practices, growth hacks, the same nine slides passed down from companies that already survived.

The two libraries almost never speak. My whole career has been one argument: close the gap between what science knows and what business does, and you stop guessing for a living. This is the book of that argument.

Close the gap, and you stop guessing for a living.
02 · THE COVER, EXPLAINED

Your brain is the hardest part

Everyone tells you the hardest part is the product. The graveyard disagrees. Three out of four venture-backed companies never return their investors' money, and most of them shipped something that worked. What they couldn't do was think clearly about their own market while every instinct they owned was lying to them.

That is the dip on the cover, the one circled in red: the moment the line stops climbing and you have to decide in the dark. Confirmation bias tells you the market loves what you already built. Sunk cost won't let you kill the campaign you're proud of. Anchoring chains you to the first number you heard.

You cannot hustle your way out of a cognitive distortion. You can only learn to catch it, and catching it is a trainable skill. Teaching that skill is most of this book.

03 · WHAT IT REFUSES TO BE

What this is, and what it refuses to be

It is not a playbook. Playbooks are reverse-engineered from the survivors, a map drawn by the lucky, for the lucky. This is a field manual for the part before you've made it, when the data is thin, the stakes are total, and every guru's advice is calibrated for a company you are not yet.

It stands on three things and shows its sources for all of them.
